## 4. Results

The invented higher-shade group records cooler surfaces and more favorable comfort ratings than the lower-shade group. Air temperature differs only slightly.

| Fictional group mean | Higher shade | Lower shade | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Shade continuity | 74% | 31% | +43 pp |
| Surface temperature | 34.8°C | 42.1°C | −7.3°C |
| Air temperature | 31.2°C | 31.8°C | −0.6°C |
| Comfort rating | 3.8 / 5 | 2.7 / 5 | +1.1 |

### 4.1 Distribution

All four higher-shade segments have a mean surface temperature below 37°C in the sample. Three of four lower-shade segments exceed 40°C. Comfort ratings vary within both groups, showing that shade does not fully explain individual response.

### 4.2 Observed sequence

The largest sample difference appears in surface temperature, followed by comfort rating. The small air-temperature difference is consistent with the thesis's emphasis on radiant and surface exposure.
