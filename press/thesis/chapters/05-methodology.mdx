## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Study design

The fictional dataset contains 96 observations: twelve observations on each of eight hypothetical street segments. Observations are evenly distributed between 11:30 and 14:30.

### 3.2 Variables

| Variable | Unit / scale | Demonstration method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shade continuity | % of walking line | Estimated from fictional site diagrams |
| Surface temperature | °C | Invented infrared reading |
| Air temperature | °C | Invented shaded sensor reading |
| Comfort rating | 1–5 | Fictional pedestrian response |

### 3.3 Grouping

Segments at or above 60% shade continuity form the higher-shade group. The remaining segments form the lower-shade group. The threshold is selected for demonstration and is not a validated standard.

### 3.4 Analysis

The example reports group means and ranges. It does not perform inferential statistics because the underlying values are invented. A real study would preregister its analysis, preserve raw observations, and report uncertainty.

### 3.5 Ethics

There were no real participants. A real pedestrian survey would require informed consent, privacy protection, an appropriate review process, and a plan for storing responses safely.
