## 5. Discussion

Within the fictional dataset, continuous shade aligns with cooler surfaces and improved comfort ratings. The pattern supports the conceptual importance of walking-route continuity: a small isolated canopy may be less useful than a sequence that protects the full trip.

### 5.1 Design interpretation

The example suggests evaluating shade at the scale of a route. Tree placement, arcade continuity, crossing exposure, and waiting areas may need to be considered together.

### 5.2 Alternative explanations

Street width, orientation, material, wind, traffic, and time of observation could produce part of the pattern. Because the observations are neither randomized nor real, the design cannot distinguish these possibilities.

### 5.3 Limitations

- Invented sample values
- Small number of hypothetical segments
- Single warm-season time window
- Simplified comfort scale
- No physiological measurements
- No inferential analysis

### 5.4 Implications for a real study

A credible follow-up would collect calibrated measurements across seasons, document shade geometry, use repeat observations, and model site-level effects. Qualitative interviews could explain why people choose shaded or exposed routes.
