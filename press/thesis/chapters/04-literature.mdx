## 2. Literature Context

Urban thermal-comfort research commonly distinguishes atmospheric conditions from radiant and surface conditions. For pedestrians, direct solar exposure can dominate short walking experiences even when nearby air-temperature readings appear similar.

### 2.1 Shade as infrastructure

Trees, arcades, awnings, and building geometry can each provide shade, but their reliability differs by season and time of day. A route with intermittent shade may produce a different experience from one with the same average coverage distributed continuously.

### 2.2 Observation and self-report

Instrument readings describe environmental conditions; comfort ratings describe an individual's response. Neither replaces the other. Clothing, pace, acclimatization, and purpose can affect self-reported comfort.
