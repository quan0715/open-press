## Question & Method

#### Starting idea

Living with a dog may create predictable routines, small responsibilities, and moments of social contact. This report asks whether students in a fictional sample describe those experiences as helpful to confidence or companionship.

### Sample design

The demonstration uses **24 invented student responses** split evenly between students who live with a dog and students who do not. Each response includes four fictional rating questions and one short comment.

| Group | Sample size | Questions |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Lives with a dog | 12 | confidence, companionship, routine, social contact |
| Does not live with a dog | 12 | confidence, companionship, routine, social contact |

### Procedure

1. Read the fictional response cards.
2. Group answers on a five-point scale.
3. Calculate a simple mean for each group.
4. Compare comments for recurring themes.

#### Limits

The sample is intentionally small and invented. It cannot establish causation, represent real students, or support a general claim about dog ownership.
