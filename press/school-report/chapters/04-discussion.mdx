## Discussion

The sample pattern is consistent with the starting idea: regular care may create routine and an immediate sense of companionship. The smaller confidence difference suggests that confidence is shaped by more than pet ownership alone.

### What the sample could mean

- Repeated care tasks may make progress visible.
- Walking a dog may create low-pressure contact with neighbors.
- A pet can offer company without requiring conversation.
- Responsibility may become stressful when time is limited.

### What it cannot mean

This fictional exercise does not show that dogs cause confidence. Family support, personality, neighborhood safety, prior experience with animals, and many other factors could shape the answers.

### A stronger real study

A real follow-up would use informed consent, a larger sample, validated survey questions, and interviews. It would compare results over time and document how animal welfare and student privacy are protected.

#### Conclusion

In this demonstration, companionship and routine are the clearest themes. The most responsible conclusion is narrow: **the invented responses show how a school report can separate observations, interpretation, and limitations.**
